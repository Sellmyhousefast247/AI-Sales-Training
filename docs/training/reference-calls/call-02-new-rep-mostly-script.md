# Reference Call #02 — New Rep, Mostly On Script, Off-Script Promises

**Outcome:** Contract signed (same call → follow-up signing later that afternoon).

**Rubric score:** 68 / 100

**Teaching label:** *Strong structural execution by a new rep — but went off-script with relocation promises and over-bonding that could create future risk.* Reps studying this call should learn what good script execution looks like AND what guardrails the rep crossed that need coaching.

---

## Scenario context

- **Rep:** Newer rep ("friend" of senior rep — referenced in the opener)
- **Lead source:** Inbound web form
- **Property:** 5 BR / 1.5 BA, ~2,100 sqft, single-family in upstate New York
- **Seller situation:** Older couple, urgent need to relocate due to wife's health (post-triple-bypass, can't handle NY winters); planning move to North Carolina; previously listed at ~$99k and pulled it down; mortgage balance ~$30k (USDA loan)
- **Pricing arc:** Seller mentioned competing offer of $60k → rep anchored cash at $33,500–$36,800 → seller wanted $50–60k → rep pivoted to novation at $50k → boss approved $58,500 → contract drafted at $58,500

---

## Step-by-step grading

### Rapport — 6/10

**Strong:**
- Used seller's name throughout
- Asked the "good time?" gate explicitly
- Casual, friendly, warm tone

**Weak:**
- Awkward intro: *"this is [Rep], I'm a friend of [Senior Rep's]"* — came across as tentative; no company name in the first 30 seconds
- Some stumbling early ("Wage Street… yep, Wade Avenue") — small loss of authority

**Key quote (positive):**
> "I just want to make sure — so you filled out an application online to sell your house. I did call you at a good time, right?"

**Key quote (weak intro):**
> "Hey, this is [Rep]. I'm a friend of [Senior Rep]'s. I was calling about your property on Wade Avenue."

---

### Motivation — 7/10

**Strong:**
- Got the WHY (wife's health, triple-bypass, can't handle winters)
- Got urgency ("need to move ASAP, well before winter")
- Got the timeline implicit (immediate)
- Asked good condition follow-ups (vinyl plank flooring, countertops, windows, roof)

**Weak:**
- Spent too long on property-tour minutiae (10+ minutes asking about wallpaper colors, countertop materials, staircase finishes). Felt like stalling.
- Didn't dig deeper into financial pain ("you mentioned you've gotta slow down on work — what's that look like for you?")

**Key quote (positive — got the WHY):**
> "My wife had triple bypass, and it's hard for her breathing winter up here in New York."

---

### Asking Price — 8/10

**Strong:**
- DID run the "how companies like ours work" educational pivot — script-correct
- Walked through the cash-vs-listing comparison
- Acknowledged the seller's pain point in real-time ("yeah, you don't have time to be signing all this stuff")

**Weak:**
- Never actually pinned the seller down on an asking price BEFORE the educational pivot. The seller mentioned $99k from the prior listing but rep didn't ask "what would you need today?"
- This was Asking Price reversed — got the education out, but the seller's number came later, organically

**Key quote (positive — script delivered correctly):**
> "We're kind of basically the opposite of a real estate agent... If you list it with a real estate agent, generally you're going to have to fix the house up... With us, we'll pay cash. We don't use traditional lending, which means you can skip that entire process including the appraisal."

---

### Trial Close 1 — 9/10

**Excellent execution.** Conditional framing + dual-benefit + verbal yes.

**Key quote:**
> "If we can get a price that works for you and works for your wife and our back office, is this something that you're ready to move forward with today?"
>
> Seller: "Yep."

The "for you and your wife" addition is good — it preempted the spousal-decision objection. **Bordered on a 10 except the rep moved past it without acknowledging the yes** ("Okay. Talk to you. Okay.") — should have anchored the yes ("Great, that's all I needed").

---

### First Hold — 7/10

**Strong:**
- Did go to hold ("just bear with me… I don't want the back office, sometimes they have a question")
- Came back and asked one more question (roof age) — back-from-hold rapport rebuild done correctly per script

**Weak:**
- Background chatter during hold was clearly transcribed (peripheral conversations) — suggests the rep didn't actually mute, or this was a "fake" hold (rep stayed in a noisy environment)
- Rep didn't fully reset rapport on return — went straight to anchor

**Key quote (return from hold):**
> "Hey bud, I forgot to ask you... on the roof, what was the last time the roof was replaced?"

---

### Anchor — 8/10

**Strong:**
- Used the back-office framing correctly ("I was back there, I was peeking over one of the ladies, [Underwriter Name]'s shoulder")
- Gave a range ("$33,500 and $36,800")
- Handled pushback well ("I'm not saying that's what they're going to pay you, that's just what I was seeing")
- Used a specific underwriter name (made it feel real)

**Weak:**
- Didn't pause after the anchor — kept talking through the seller's reaction
- Gave a SECOND number ($40k cash max) right after the anchor, which broke the back-office framing and felt like negotiating against himself

**Key quote (positive — strong anchor framing):**
> "I was back there, and I was peeking over one of the ladies, [Underwriter Name]'s shoulder. And what I could see was they were somewhere in between $33,500 and $36,800. That was wholesale. That's where they were at."

**Key quote (weak — second number undercut the anchor):**
> "The best I could do for cash offer would be $40."

---

### Negotiation — 7/10

**Strong:**
- Used the value reset ("if I could make it super easy for you, just buy your property as-is... no commission, no fees, we cover all the closing costs, closing on your timeline... what's the best you can do?")
- Pivoted to novation cleanly when cash didn't work
- Walked through the novation program description per script

**Weak:**
- Seller never gave a clean "best you could do" number — the rep let the seller's $30k mortgage payoff become the conversation focus rather than a target sale price
- The "as investors, our goal is to make a marginal profit" speech was rushed
- **Started inventing additional services mid-negotiation** ("we can also help you find a house in North Carolina") — this expanded the deal beyond what was vetted

**Key quote (positive — value reset):**
> "If I could make it super easy for you, just buy your property as-is, meaning you don't have to do anything... what do you think the best you could do is?"

**Key quote (weak — invented promises):**
> "We can do all that... we're going to do everything we can. We want to make sure that we get this deal."

---

### Trial Close 2 — 4/10

**Mostly skipped.** After the novation pitch, the rep slid into "give me a couple hours and I'll send the contract" instead of asking the conditional close: *"If I can get this approved, you ready to move forward right?"*

**Key quote (what happened):**
> "Let me see here. I'm thinking the program... we could get you about 50."

**What it should have been:**
> "Let me see if I can get the novation approved at $50k. I gotta grab a couple of people in my office to help me. But if I can — you ready to move forward right?"

The trial close was implied but never explicitly asked. Got an implicit yes ("we'll think about it over") which is NOT a clean yes.

---

### Second Hold — 6/10

**Strong:**
- Did go to hold for novation approval
- Came back with the boss's approved number ($58,500) — that's an upgrade over the $50k floated initially

**Weak:**
- The hold got contaminated with the seller's relocation request ("can you help us find a house in NC?") — rep agreed to this side-deal mid-hold, complicating the agreement
- Background chatter during hold was again audible (multiple voices, "Captain Morgan" references) — feels like rep is in a noisy environment, not a real strategic huddle
- Hold didn't deliver a tight cash-vs-novation decision — rep had already pivoted before the hold

---

### Approval / Close — 6/10

**Strong:**
- Did get the "approved" framing ("my boss is right here... we can get you $58,500")
- Did set up the document/email logistics (asked for emails, asked for spouse's name, structured the contract)
- Did address the wife's anxiety (when she expressed fear about signing without a home secured)

**Weak:**
- **Did NOT close in-call.** Punted to a 3:00–3:15 PM callback to send contracts. Lost the "now" momentum.
- Over-promised on the relocation help ("we'll help you buy a house," "if you need 5 months we'll give you 5 months," "we're like family now") — these are not in the script and may create legal/operational liability
- Used "I love you" in the close — way too familiar, broke professional frame
- Wife's anxiety was deflected ("don't worry, we're family") instead of addressed with the actual contract mechanics (closing date flexibility, amendment options, contingencies)

**Key quote (problematic over-promise):**
> "If you need 5 months, we're going to give you 5 months. Don't worry about it... once we get this contract signed, we're going to take care of you. We're like family now."

**Key quote (problematic close):**
> "All right, guys. I love you. Be looking for that house. Yep. Thank you. Bye-bye."

---

## What this call did right (a new rep success story)

Three things worth celebrating:

1. **Hit the major structural beats.** Trial Close 1 with conditional framing → first hold → back-office anchor with named underwriter → novation pivot when cash didn't work → second hold → approval framing. That's the script.
2. **Got the contract signed.** Outcome matters. Despite the rough edges, the rep moved a motivated seller to commitment.
3. **Adapted in real-time.** When the seller revealed the spousal relocation need, the rep didn't freeze — pulled in the boss, expanded the conversation, kept momentum.

This is what you want from a new rep.

---

## What needs coaching

Three priority coaching moments for this rep's next 1:1:

1. **Off-script promises are a liability.** "We can help you buy a house" and "if you need 5 months we'll give you 5 months" are commitments the company hasn't approved. If we can't deliver, this becomes a legal and reputational issue. Coaching: every additional service has to come from the senior rep / boss explicitly, not from the rep ad-libbing.

2. **Wife's anxiety was a missed moment.** When the spouse said *"I'm terrified if we're signing contracts and then we don't get a house and then we're stuck losing this house,"* the rep deflected. The right move is to validate ("totally fair concern") + walk through the actual contract mechanics: closing date, possession agreements, amendment options, contingencies. *Mechanics calm anxiety; assurance amplifies it.*

3. **Always close in-call when possible.** The rep had Trial Close 2 momentum but punted to a 3:15 callback. Every gap between yes and signature is a chance for the deal to fall apart. The script's Approval section is built to keep momentum — use it.
