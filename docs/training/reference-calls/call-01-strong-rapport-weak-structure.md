# Reference Call #01 — Strong Rapport, Weak Structure

**Outcome:** Contract signed by seller one week after this call → deal closed at $180,000 for both parcels.

**Rubric score:** 40 / 100

**Teaching label:** *Successful by relationship; failed by process.* The rep relied on rapport to close a deal that should have been closed via the structured Road to a Deal flow. Reps studying this call should learn what was strong about the rapport AND what would have made the call score 80+ instead of 40.

---

## Scenario context

- **Lead source:** Inbound web form submission
- **Property:** Two parcels (manufactured home + adjacent vacant lot) in rural Florida
- **Seller situation:** Out-of-state heir; father recently passed; sole heir; pre-probate (judge expected to sign in June); seller is cognitively overwhelmed by the workload
- **Asking price journey:** Seller mentioned realtor had previously listed at $340k → seller said she'd accept $230k → seller floated $230k → rep landed at $180k for both parcels

---

## Step-by-step grading

### Rapport — 7/10

**Strong:**
- Tone was warm and genuinely curious throughout
- Built real connection: shared backgrounds (Chicago / San Antonio / Athens, Georgia)
- Followed seller's lead on small talk — peacocks, guns, orange trees — without forcing it back to business

**Weak:**
- No formal intro for nearly 4 minutes. "It's [Rep]. Sorry, we're playing phone tag" is informal — fine for a callback, but didn't include the company name early.
- No "good time?" gate at the top. Seller had to volunteer she was busy ("we're both busy people").

**Key quote (positive):**
> "I'm from Chicago originally. I live in San Antonio now, but we buy houses nationwide."

**Key quote (negative — informal opener):**
> "Hi, [Seller]? — Hi. — Hey. It's [Rep]. Sorry, we're playing phone tag."

---

### Motivation — 6/10

**Strong:**
- Rep listened more than talked
- Got the WHY (inherited, lives out of state, just got promoted, "want this off my chest")
- Got the timeline (probate clears June, flying down 21st-28th, July 1 close)
- Got the property condition (manufactured home + vacant lot + utilities hooked up, vacant)

**Weak:**
- The seller volunteered most of this — rep did not actively *dig* into motivation
- Didn't ask follow-up questions to deepen pain (e.g., "what's the hardest part of this for you?" / "what would it mean to you to have this off your plate?")

**Key quote (positive — seller volunteered motivation):**
> "I really gotta get away. I want this headache off my head."

---

### Asking Price — 3/10

**Major miss.** The rep asked for the seller's asking price, but **completely skipped the "how companies like ours work" comparison vs listing with an agent.** This is a critical structural omission — it leaves the seller with no framework for why our number is different from "market value."

**Key quote (the question was asked):**
> "What price would you be content with? We wanna make sure that you're happy and your needs are met."

**What was missing — the script's required educational pivot:**
> "Are you familiar with how companies like ours work? Well, we're basically the opposite of a real estate agent. So, there's two different ways that you can sell a property: to a company like ours, or you can list it with a real estate agent. If you list it with a real estate agent, generally you're gonna have to fix the house up... With us, since we are paying cash, we can skip that entire process and close in 2 or 3 weeks."

The rep eventually mentioned "we cover closing costs, take houses as-is, no inspections, no appraisals" — but in fragments and AFTER the price was on the table, not as the structured educational pivot before it.

---

### Trial Close 1 — 0/10

**Skipped entirely.** The rep never asked the conditional move-forward question:

> "If we can get to a price that works for you AND works for our back office, is this something you want to move forward with today?"

The deal moved into negotiation without that gate — which is why the call lost structure later.

---

### First Hold — 0/10

**Skipped entirely.** No hold, no "let me check with underwriting," no return-from-hold rapport rebuild. The rep had no anchor moment to set up.

---

### Anchor — 4/10

**Soft anchor in the rep's own voice — not back-office framing.**

The rep said:
> "I think we'll need to be in the probably low 2 hundreds maybe once it's fixed up. I'm still doing research."

**Why 4:**
- A range was implied ("low 2 hundreds")
- BUT stated by the rep, not as a back-office estimate
- BUT no 3+ second pause — the rep kept talking
- BUT prefixed with hedging ("I think... maybe... I'm still doing research") which kills authority

**What it should have been:**
> "(downward inflection) So, when I was back there, I was peeking over one of the ladies' shoulders, and it looks like they're coming back somewhere between $135,000 and $150,000…"
>
> *(5-second silence)*

The seller's response — "What??!!" — should have been the anchor's moment. Instead the rep walked her through the math himself.

---

### Negotiation — 6/10

**Mixed.** The rep eventually used some value bullets ("we cover closing costs, take houses as-is, you don't have to deal with inspections / appraisals / fix-ups") but they came in fragments rather than the structured value reset before asking for the seller's best number.

**Key quote (partial value reset):**
> "We're like the easy button. We handle everything. We cover closing costs. We take houses as-is."

**The seller's number came naturally:**
> "I wouldn't expect market value... I think you could do a little bit better, especially if you put some money into it."

→ Seller floated $230. Rep ran the math at 70% × $240 = $168, then offered $180.

The negotiation worked, but more from rapport ("I want this headache off my head") than from the structured "what do you think the best you could do is?" framing.

---

### Trial Close 2 — 7/10

**Got there, but informally.**

> "If we agreed on price, you'd be feel good about going ahead and doing the contract with us, and we can move on?"
>
> Seller: "Absolutely. I would. Of course, I would."

That counts as a Trial Close 2 with a clean verbal yes. Conditional framing was present ("if we agreed on price"), though without the canonical "if I can get this approved" framing.

---

### Second Hold — 0/10

**Skipped entirely.** No hold to decide cash vs. novation. The rep went straight from the seller's $180 acceptance to "let me crunch numbers and call you tomorrow" — and then sent the contract that same call when the seller pushed.

This skip cost the rep the chance to anchor lower OR pivot to novation if needed.

---

### Approval / Close — 7/10

**Closed the contract by the end of the call — but without the script's ceremony.**

**What was strong:**
- Rep did walk through the email/document logistics
- Rep got verbal commitment to sign the contract that same evening (or follow-up call next morning)
- Rep set up clean handoff: "we'll talk tomorrow at 10:30 AM"
- Outcome: contract signed by seller one week later

**What was missing — the "approved for our portfolio" framing:**
> "[Seller], I have some great news for you. I just got out of underwriting and I've got your property approved for our portfolio. Which is actually the first one I've gotten approved all week..."

Instead the rep said something more along the lines of "let me crunch the numbers and call you tomorrow" — then sent the contract when the seller insisted she was ready now. The seller carried the close, not the rep.

**Key quote (seller carrying the close):**
> "I want this headache off my head. Okay. I'm just gonna do it. I'm just gonna send it over. Send me that contract right now, I'll sign it. I promise you. I will."

A rep who'd run the script would have *led* the seller to that moment — anchor low, get the counter, value-reset, get yes-to-proceed, hold to "approve," come back with excitement. Here the rep arrived at the same destination but largely because the seller was so fatigued she pushed for the close herself.

---

## Why this call closed despite the rubric gaps

Three factors made this a winnable call regardless of process:

1. **High motivation.** Seller was emotionally and logistically overwhelmed (recent loss, busy job, out-of-state, no family help). She was ready to be done.
2. **Low expectation.** Seller didn't expect market value ("I wouldn't expect market value... assessment is $230, I'd take that"). Her anchor was below market.
3. **Genuine rapport.** Rep was warm, curious, not pushy. Built trust through small-talk windows the seller opened (peacocks, guns, family).

**The lesson:** Easy deals will close even with skipped steps. Hard deals — uncertain seller, well-priced property, multiple competing offers — require the structure. A rep who has *only* the relational style will lose the harder calls.

---

## What this call should have looked like at 80/100

Same outcome, tighter execution. Annotated version:

1. **Rapport (10):** Start with company name and a "good time?" gate. *"Hey [Seller], it's [Rep] with Sell My House Fast — got a quick minute?"*
2. **Motivation (10):** Same listening, but actively dig with one or two follow-ups: *"Out of curiosity, what's the part you'd most like off your plate?"*
3. **Asking Price (10):** Run the educational pivot BEFORE the price talk. *"Real quick — are you familiar with how companies like ours work?"* → walk through the comparison.
4. **Trial Close 1 (10):** Before getting into pricing math: *"If we can get to a price that works for you AND our back office, you ready to move forward today?"*
5. **First Hold (10):** *"Let me put you on a brief hold and check with underwriting."*
6. **Anchor (10):** Come back: *"They're coming back somewhere between $135,000 and $150,000..."* → 5 seconds of silence.
7. **Negotiation (10):** *"If I can buy as-is — no commissions, no fees, we cover closing, your timeline — what's the best you could do?"* → seller counters at $230.
8. **Trial Close 2 (10):** *"Let me see if I can make $230 work. I gotta grab a couple people to help me. If I can, you ready to move forward right?"*
9. **Second Hold (10):** Real strategic decision — cash at $135 or novation closer to $200?
10. **Approval (10):** *"[Seller], I've got great news for you — got your property approved for our portfolio... Got a pen and paper handy?..."*

Same close. Same outcome. But the rep would have anchored lower (potentially closing at $135-150 instead of $180), AND the seller would have closed *that night*, not a week later.

**Estimated value left on the table: $30,000–$45,000.**
