# Reference Call #03 — Same Rep, Off-Script, Deal Pending

**Outcome:** No contract signed in-call. Rep texted two options (cash $45k or novation $60k) and scheduled a Monday follow-up. Final outcome unknown as of this transcript.

**Rubric score:** 33 / 100

**Teaching label:** *Same rep as Call #02 — but visibly worse. The disconnect/reconnect was mishandled, the anchor was confused with three competing numbers, the seller's counter was never pulled, and the close was punted to "I'll call you Monday." Useful low-end anchor for the AI scorer.*

---

## Scenario context

- **Rep:** Same new rep as Call #02 (about a week later)
- **Lead source:** Inbound — call had disconnected, this is the recall
- **Property:** ~1,600 sqft single-family in coastal Maine; vacant; previous appraisal $100k+; needs $50-60k in renovations (windows, doors, flooring, sheetrock, kitchen, plumbing); copper pipes were stripped by intruders
- **Seller situation:** Already relocated, just needs the cash to fund projects in his new location; ASAP timeline
- **Pricing arc:** Seller asking $125k → cash anchor $40-45k → comparable sale shown on Zillow at $114k → novation offered at $60k → seller said he'd think about it → rep punted to Monday callback

## Step-by-step grading

### Rapport — 2/10

**The call started as a recall after a disconnect.** This was a major rapport reset opportunity. The rep wasted it.

**Weak:**
- No "hey we got cut off, sorry about that — you have a minute to keep going?" reset
- No company name reintroduction
- No rapport rebuild — went straight back into questioning ("And time frame, what did you say?")
- Worse, at the END of the call, rep called the seller by his last name ("Leon") then corrected to first name ("Peter") — signal he wasn't tracking who he was talking to

**Key quote (poor reset):**
> "So we were back on — give me a sec. I had everything here nice and going. And time frame — what did you say that you wanted to move out, how quick?"

**What it should have been:**
> "Hey [Seller], sorry — looks like we got cut off there. Service okay? Got a minute to keep going?"

### Motivation — 4/10

**Strong:**
- Got the WHY ("already relocated, need money to do projects where I'm at now")
- Got urgency ("ASAP")
- Confirmed vacancy

**Weak:**
- Didn't probe the relocation pain at all — what kind of projects? what's the urgency really about?
- Skipped the timeline question structurally — got "ASAP" but didn't ask "what does ASAP mean to you — 2 weeks? 4? 6?"
- Drilled into property condition (windows, plumbing, electrical) but in a list-fashion rather than letting the seller talk about it

**Key quote:**
> "I'm already relocated. I just need the money to do the projects where I'm at now."

That was the entire motivation extraction.

### Asking Price — 6/10

**Strong:**
- Asked directly: *"What are you trying to get out of it? What are you trying to walk away with?"* → Got "$125k"
- Did run the "how companies like ours work" educational pivot afterward

**Weak:**
- The educational pivot felt rushed — read more like memorized than internalized
- No follow-up dialog on what the seller thought of the comparison

### Trial Close 1 — 7/10

**Strong:**
- Asked the conditional close: *"If we can come up with some numbers that work for you and work for our back office, is it something you're ready to sign off today?"*
- Got verbal yes: *"Anytime."*

**Weak:**
- Wording stumbled ("sign sign off")
- Didn't lock in the yes — moved on quickly

### First Hold — 5/10

**Strong:**
- Did go to hold

**Weak:**
- The hold appears to have been **roughly 8 minutes long** based on the transcript timestamps (8:09 → 16:45) — that's an unusually long hold for a "back office quick check." Holds longer than ~3 minutes start losing the seller.
- Return-from-hold rapport rebuild was thin: just asked one more property-condition question (windows) before anchoring

### Anchor — 4/10

**Strong:**
- Used the back-office framing with named underwriter ("peeking over Jennifer's shoulder")
- Initial range was sound: *"somewhere between 40 and 45 k"*

**Weak:**
- **Gave three competing numbers** in succession:
  > "It looks like they're gonna be somewhere between 40 and 45 k... We're looking at about 35, $40, $45 at the most cash."
  
  That's a 35–45 range stated three different ways, which dilutes authority and signals uncertainty. The seller hears "the rep doesn't know."
- Didn't pause after the anchor — kept talking
- Backpedaled into "I'm not saying that's what they're gonna pay you" too quickly (without seller pushing back first)

### Negotiation — 3/10

This is where the call really fell apart.

**Weak:**
- Started the value reset correctly: *"if we can make it super easy for you... buy the property as-is... no commissions, no fees, cover all costs, close on your timeline."*
- **But never asked the seller's counter** ("what's the best you could do?") — the most important question in the Negotiation step
- Instead, the rep pivoted to **showing Zillow comparables live during the call**:
  > "Right here on Zillow... 25 Pleasant Street, Lubeck, that one closed for 110... 1 Country Road, two bed one bath, sold for 114,500... It's exactly like your house..."
- This is off-script and a strategic mistake. The whole point of the back-office framing is that the numbers come FROM the back office, not from the rep's screen. Pulling up Zillow undercuts the authority the back-office framing was supposed to build.
- Never gave the seller a chance to give his own counter number, so the rep has no idea where the seller would actually have landed

### Trial Close 2 — 0/10

**Skipped entirely.** No conditional close, no "if I can get this approved, you ready to move forward right?"

The rep slid directly from the novation pitch into "you want me to text you those two options?" That's not a trial close — that's a punt.

**What it should have been:**
> "Let me see if I can get the novation approved at $60k. I gotta grab a couple people in my office to help me. If I can — you ready to move forward right?"

### Second Hold — 0/10

**No second hold.** The cash-vs-novation decision was made on the fly, with the seller hearing both options simultaneously. No strategic pause to let the seller weigh.

### Approval / Close — 2/10

**Major failure point.** The rep had a yes from Trial Close 1 and a soft interest in novation, and instead of closing in-call, he:

1. Tried to send the contract via email
2. Discovered the seller couldn't access his email (forgot password, son couldn't help)
3. Pivoted to texting two options instead of a single contract
4. Asked the seller for a "good email to send contract" later
5. Punted to a Monday callback
6. Ended by calling the seller "Leon" (his last name) then correcting to "Peter"

**Key quote (the punt):**
> "I'm gonna call you back Monday. And then we'll see where you're at then, and then we'll go from there."

A seller with no urgency reset who's been told he can compare two options on his own time is a seller who's likely to:
- Get cold feet over the weekend
- Get a higher offer from another investor
- Forget the conversation
- Decide to think about it longer

**What should have happened:**
- "Approved for our portfolio" excitement framing for the novation at $60k
- "Pen and paper handy?" walk-through
- Address the email problem in-call ("no problem, let's text it instead — what's your phone number? Pulling it up now")
- Get the signature in-call OR get a firm commitment for the same day callback (not Monday)

## What this call shows the AI scorer

This call is the **clearest "low-end anchor"** of the three reference calls so far. Three concrete teaching moments:

1. **Reconnects after a disconnect need an explicit rapport reset.** Going right back into questions signals "I'm just running a script, not having a conversation."
2. **Three competing anchor numbers ≠ one strong anchor.** "$40 to $45... 35 to 45 at the most" is worse than just "$40 to $45." Pick one range and stand on it.
3. **Skipping the seller's counter is a Negotiation step failure, not just a missed opportunity.** Without the counter, the rep is anchoring against himself with Zillow comps — and once you start showing the seller comps live, the back-office framing is gone.

## What this rep needs in 1:1 coaching

Three priorities, in order:

1. **Single-range anchor, hold the silence.** Practice anchoring with ONE range. Pause for 3+ seconds. Don't backpedal until the seller pushes back. Roleplay this 10 times.

2. **Always ask the seller's counter.** "What's the best you could do?" comes BEFORE showing any comp data. Never pull up Zillow on a live call.

3. **Close in-call or get a same-day callback.** "I'll call Monday" lets the deal die. If the seller has an email problem, solve it in real time (text the contract; switch to phone-friendly e-sign; get on a screen-share). Don't punt for 4 days.

If the rep nails just these three, his next call will likely be 60+ even with the other gaps.
