## PART 1 — MASTER SCRIPT (Road to a Deal)

### Step 1 — Rapport

Rep (upward inflection): "(seller's name)?"
Seller: "Yes."

Rep (downward inflection): "Hey (seller's name), (rep's name), how are you? (upward inflection)"
Seller: "Good, who are you?"

Rep (downward inflection): "(seller's name), I was calling your property on (street name)…"

If seller stays neutral or says "Okay…":
  (continue without explanation)

If seller asks "Do I know you?" or "How'd you get my number?":
  Rep (downward inflection): "Looks like you had just filled out our form online… did I call you at a good time? (upward inflection)"

Seller: "Yes."


### Step 2 — Motivation

Rep (slowly, sound confused): "Ok great, looks like I have a little bit of information here…"
(pause)
Rep: "I guess, um, tell me what's going on? (upward inflection)"

(Focus more on the situation than the property at first.)

Seller: "(blah blah blah — gives reason)"

Rep: (digs deep into motivation — keep asking follow-up questions until you understand the why)

Rep: "What's your ideal timeline?"
Seller: "(gives timeline)"

Rep: "Ok, well, can you tell me about the property?"
Seller: "(gives some information)"

Rep: (gets the rest of the major information: age of roof, HVAC, foundation, condition of house, updates, etc.)
Seller: "(answers)"


### Step 3 — Asking Price

Rep: "Ok so, how much do you think you would want for the house?"

If seller gives a dollar amount:
  Seller: "(dollar amount)"

If seller deflects:
  Seller: "Not sure…"

Rep: "Great! Are you familiar with how companies like ours work?"

(Proceed regardless of yes/no/maybe.)
Seller: "Yes" / "No" / "Maybe so"

Rep: "Well, we are basically the opposite of a real estate agent. So, there's two different ways that you can sell a property: to a company like ours, or you can list it with a real estate agent. If you list it with a real estate agent, generally you're gonna have to fix the house up, possibly paint it, etc. Every time they have a showing you're gonna have to clean up and make your bed and leave the house, and that could go on for a while, while you get offers. Then you negotiate offers until you accept one. Those folks typically have to get approved for a mortgage and that takes some time and also requires your house to have an appraisal. Once that's done, then you set up the closing — and that whole process from start to finish is usually about three or four months long."

(pause)

Rep: "With us, since we are paying cash, we don't use traditional lending — which means that we can skip that entire process including the appraisal — and so in many situations we can get it closed within 2 or 3 weeks."

Rep (with sincerity): "And so, (seller's name)… my goal… is to make this as easy for you as possible…"
(emphasize the word EASY)


### Step 4 — Trial Close 1

Rep: "So, if we can get to a price that works for you and works for our back office, is this something you want to move forward with today? (upward inflection)"

(MUST GET A YES TO MOVE FORWARD.)

Seller: "Yes."

Rep: "Okay great. Let me place you on a brief hold and see if the underwriting department has any other questions."


### Step 5 — First Hold

(Place seller on hold.)
(Take seller off hold.)

Rep: "Hey guys, are you there? Ok cool — they did have a couple of additional questions about the house…"

(Now you can ask anything you need or forgot, or just use some throwaway questions. Take your time with this part — it puts you back into rapport after the hold.)


### Step 6 — Anchor

Rep (downward inflection): "So, when I was back there, I was peeking over one of the ladies' shoulders… and it looks like they're gonna be coming back somewhere between (give dollar amount)…"

(You must pause after the anchor to gauge their response. After you say this, shut up and stay silent.)

Seller: "What??!!"

If seller pushes back:
  Rep: "Oh, I'm not saying that that's what we're going to pay you — that's just what I was seeing when I was back there…"
  Seller: "Oh, ok."


### Step 7 — Negotiation

Rep (downward inflection): "Well Mrs. Seller, let me ask you — if I could make it super easy for you, and buy your property as-is, meaning you don't need to do anything to it, and there would be no commission and no fees, we cover all the closing costs, and close it on your timeline… what do you think the best you could do is? (upward inflection)"

Seller: "(gives dollar amount)"


### Step 8 — Trial Close 2

Rep: "Gotcha, ok. Let me go back there and see if I can make that happen for you. I need to grab a couple of people in my office to help me work on this and see if that's a price we can do. I'm not sure if it is… But if we can, are you ready to move forward with it right? (upward inflection)"

(MUST GET A YES TO MOVE FORWARD.)


### Step 9 — Second Hold

(Place seller on hold.)
(Take seller off hold.)

(NOTE: At this point we decide whether to close as a cash deal or switch to novation. If cash deal, go straight to Approval. If novation, run the Novation section below, then Approval.)


### Novation Path (alternate route to Approval — only if cash won't work)

Rep (downward inflection): "So, they're coming back in the range of (give dollar amount). Yeah, I know that won't work for you. I've been telling them that, and they understand that you need to be in the (give dollar amount) range (with sincerity)…"

Rep (downward inflection): "Unfortunately, we cannot make that work because, as investors, our goal is to make a marginal profit. And you know, we've been doing this for a long time, and based on what we can sell it for once it's fixed up and the amount of money we would have to put into it to get it in that condition… It's all based on a formula. And unfortunately all the companies like ours, we all use the same formula to arrive at what we can pay for it. Does that make sense? (upward inflection)"

(Really big pause here. Go slow leading into the next part, unless they are trying to jump off the call.)

Rep: "Now, having said that — we do have another program. If I could get you guys approved for it, that would take a little longer… But… we could get you what you are needing to get for the property (with sincerity)…"

Seller: "Oh really, what is that?"

Rep: "So this program — if we could get it approved — would be a good fit for you. The customers we do this for love it, and it would get you (give dollar amount). That's going to be our concierge service, or retail buyer program."

Rep: "This program is basically a partnership where we take all the hassle and responsibilities off your plate. What we do is work with our network of buyers, agents, and contractors to get this done. We'll find the end buyer that will move into the property. And you guys don't have to do anything. The best part is — all the things that get called out in the inspection and/or appraisal that need to be repaired or replaced, we will pay for that stuff. We'll do all the heavy lifting. And this way, you get what you're asking for the property, and you don't have to do anything or pay for anything in the meantime…"

Seller: "That might work for us…"

Rep: "Ok, well let me know if you want me to see if we can make this happen. Do you have any other questions about it? (upward inflection)"


### Novation Trial Close

Rep: "Ok, let me see if I can make this happen — and I gotta get a different person involved. So if I can, you wanna move forward, right?"

(MUST GET A YES TO MOVE FORWARD.)


### Step 10 — Approval / Close

Rep (with excitement): "(Seller's name), ok so I have some great news for you. I just got out of the underwriting department in the back, and… I've got your property approved for our portfolio! Which is actually the first one I've gotten approved all week. Do you have a pen and paper handy? I've got some info for you to write down."

Seller: "Ok, yes."

Rep: "Ok, so what we have done is put funds aside so that no one else in the office can spend those funds on any other property. On the day of the closing, those funds will be transferred to you. Is your email address (seller's email address)? Do you have access to it right now?"

Seller: "Ok, yes."

Rep: "Ok great. So, I'm actually surprised because they approved your property for more than what I thought they would. With us paying all the closing costs, and with zero fees and zero commissions, we got you approved for (dollar amount). Now, I'm sending you an email right now — let me know when you get that."

Seller: "Ok, I got it."

Rep: "Ok, let me know when you can open it up."

Seller: "Ok, I can see it."

Rep: "Ok, so the main things I want you to make sure are correct: your name, the price, the address — we don't want to accidentally buy your neighbor's house, haha."

Rep: "Just sign at the bottom."

(Seller confirms signing.)

Rep: "Ok, just a few things for you. Someone from the closing department will reach out to you, probably tomorrow. They'll exchange info with you and start being your main point of contact. It'll be (name) — maybe; I'm not sure yet. They'll get with you about photos and access."

Seller: "Ok."

Rep: "Do you have any questions for me right now?"
Seller: "Nope."

Rep: "Ok, cool. Well, save my number so if you need me you can call me. And be looking for a call from a (give area code) — that'll be your closing coordinator."

Seller: "Great, thank you."

Rep: (says goodbye)

## PART 2 — COMMON OBJECTIONS & REBUTTALS

(These are flexible templates aligned to our voice — reps shouldn't read them verbatim, but the moves should match.)

### "Send me the offer in writing first"
Seller: "Just email me the offer."
Rep (calm, sincere): "Totally fair. The thing is — the number won't be right until I've gone through the details with you on the phone. Let me get us to a number you'd actually accept, and then send you the paperwork. Sound good? (upward inflection)"

### "I need to talk to my spouse"
Seller: "I need to run this by my husband/wife first."
Rep (warm): "Of course. Are they around right now? If you can grab them — even for a couple minutes — we can answer everything on this call and save you the back-and-forth. (pause) If not, what's the best time we can get them on?"

### "I want to think about it"
Seller: "Let me sleep on it."
Rep (curious, not pushy): "Yeah, that makes sense. Just so I understand — what's the part you're thinking about? Is it the number, the process, something else? (upward inflection)"
(Goal: surface the real objection. "Think about it" almost always masks something specific.)

### "Your offer is too low"
Seller: "That's nowhere near what I want."
Rep (firm, sincere): "I hear you, and I understand. The number isn't going to work as a cash deal — and I appreciate you being straight with me. (pause) Let me ask — if there was a way to get you closer to your number without you fixing anything or paying any fees, would that be worth exploring? (upward inflection)"
(Goal: pivot to novation.)

### "I'm getting other offers"
Seller: "I have other companies looking too."
Rep (matter-of-fact): "Smart — I'd do the same. Most of those folks are running the same formula we are, so the numbers usually land in the same range. What's different is who actually closes — half of those guys back out the day before and re-trade you on price. (pause) If I get you approved, the number doesn't move."

### "Why so low? How'd you get that number?"
Seller: "Why so low?"
Rep (transparent): "Great question. We work it backwards — what we can sell it for once it's fixed up, minus what it costs to fix, minus what we need to make. Every company like ours uses the same math. That's also how I know whether novation could get you closer to your number, since that doesn't require us to fix it."

### "I'd rather list with an agent"
Seller: "I'm just going to list it."
Rep (no pushback): "That's totally legitimate. Just so you know what to expect — most agents will want some prep work and the timeline's 3–4 months. If that fits your situation, go for it. (pause) Out of curiosity, what's making you lean toward listing?"

### "Call me back next week"
Seller: "Call me Monday."
Rep (curious): "Happy to. Can I ask — what changes between now and then? Is there a piece of info you're waiting on, or is it more about timing? (upward inflection)"

### "I'm not signing anything today"
Seller: "I won't sign today."
Rep (no pressure): "Totally up to you. The reason we sign today is to lock in the number — otherwise we re-run underwriting later and the price can move. (pause) If that's not a fit, no hard feelings."

### "Zillow says my house is worth more"
Seller: "Zestimate says $X."
Rep (calm, educational): "Zillow's a great starting point — the issue is, Zillow doesn't see your house. It averages recent sales in your zip and assumes yours is in the same condition. We actually look at your property and compare it to houses that sold in the same condition. That's why our number's usually different."

### "I owe more than that on the mortgage"
Seller: "I owe more than that to the bank."
Rep (sincere): "I appreciate you telling me. That changes the conversation a little — let me ask a few questions to see if there's still a path. Do you know roughly what the loan balance is?"
(Goal: assess short-sale or novation viability.)

### "Is this a scam?"
Seller: "How do I know this is legit?"
Rep (steady): "Totally fair to ask. We're (company name), based in (city). Look us up — Better Business Bureau, Google reviews, all of it. Every closing goes through a licensed title company, so they hold the funds and verify everything. You never pay us; we pay you at closing."

## PART 3 — VOICEMAIL SCRIPT

(When seller doesn't answer.)

Rep (warm, casual): "Hey (seller's name), this is (rep's name) calling about the property on (street name). Looks like you filled out our form online — had a couple quick questions about the house, hoping to get you a number. Give me a call back at (rep's number). Thanks!"

**Rules:**
- Keep it under 20 seconds.
- Don't pitch on voicemail.
- Don't promise a price or terms.
- Just create a callback hook.

## PART 4 — UNIVERSAL RULES

These rules apply across the whole call, regardless of which step is active:

- **Confirm the seller's name before pitching anything.** Never lead with "I want to buy your house." Always lead with their name + a question.
- **Never give a price without going to hold first.** Numbers said off-the-cuff lose authority. Even when the rep knows the number, they go to hold and "come back" with it.
- **Always pause after the anchor.** First person to break silence loses leverage. After saying the number, shut up — even if it's awkward.
- **Get a verbal yes at every Trial Close.** A "maybe," "we'll see," or anything non-yes counts as NO. Don't move forward without a clean yes.
- **Never argue with the seller.** When they push back, agree → validate → redirect. "I hear you. That doesn't work as a cash deal. Let's see what does."
- **Never give the seller homework.** "Go check Zillow," "find your tax bill," "talk to your accountant" — each one breaks the conversation and you lose them.
- **Never give specific dollar repair costs.** Don't say "the roof needs $15k." Talk in ranges or totals.
- **Use the seller's name multiple times** — at minimum once per step. Builds rapport, signals attentiveness.
- **If they say they're driving, ask them to pull over.** A distracted seller is a no-deal seller.

## PART 5 — SCORING SCALE & PER-STEP RUBRIC

### Scoring Scale (0–10 integer per step)

The AI may use any integer from 0 through 10. Anchors below describe what the key grades mean. Intermediates (1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 9) are allowed for in-between cases.

```
10 = Executed perfectly. Followed the script flow, tone, and framing.
 9 = Near-perfect. One micro-issue (tone slipped briefly, etc.).
 8 = Executed well. Minor misstep that didn't damage the call.
 7 = Solid execution with a meaningful gap or rough edge.
 6 = More than halfway there but a real piece is missing.
 5 = Attempted with partial success. Some critical pieces missing.
 4 = Attempted but mostly ineffective.
 3 = Attempted but counterproductive — could argue it didn't really happen.
 2 = Brief mention or empty gesture.
 1 = Token attempt; effectively skipped.
 0 = Not done. No evidence in the transcript.
```

**Final call score = sum of all 10 step scores. Out of 100.**

### Per-Step Rubric

For each step, 10/7/5/3/0 anchors are listed. Use intermediate values as needed.

#### Rapport
- 10 = Confirmed seller's name; warm intro; "good time?" check; seller engaged and reciprocating.
- 7 = Most boxes checked; tone slightly cold or rushed.
- 5 = Got name and intro but skipped one critical piece (permission / warmth / inflection).
- 3 = Identified themselves but immediately pivoted to pitch.
- 0 = No intro OR so cold the seller pushed back.

#### Motivation
- 10 = Open-ended "what's going on?" → got the WHY, the timeline, AND property condition. Rep listened more than talked.
- 7 = Got most of it; one piece was thin.
- 5 = Got partial info — usually property but missed the why or timeline.
- 3 = Surface-level questions only; never dug.
- 0 = Skipped motivation; jumped to price.

#### Asking Price
- 10 = Asked seller's number AND walked through the "how companies like ours work" comparison.
- 7 = Asked the price question; comparison was thin or skipped.
- 5 = Asked for asking price but didn't get a number OR didn't educate.
- 3 = Skirted the price question without asking it directly.
- 0 = Never asked what the seller wanted.

#### Trial Close 1
- 10 = Conditional framing ("if we can get to a price that works for you AND our back office") + verbal YES.
- 7 = Asked move-forward but missed the dual-benefit framing.
- 5 = Got a soft "I guess so" or "maybe" rather than a clean yes.
- 3 = Asked something close but seller never confirmed forward intent.
- 0 = Skipped — went straight to anchor or hold without trial close.

#### First Hold
- 10 = Real hold (10+ seconds); back-from-hold rapport rebuild before anchoring.
- 7 = Did the hold but transition back was abrupt.
- 5 = Brief hold; no real rapport rebuild on return.
- 3 = "Hold" was instant — no actual pause.
- 0 = No hold whatsoever.

#### Anchor
- 10 = "They're coming back somewhere between $X" — downward inflection — 3+ second pause — handled pushback without backpedaling on the framing.
- 7 = Anchored well but pause was short OR backpedal was weak.
- 5 = Stated a number as the offer (not as a back-office estimate). OR no pause.
- 3 = Mentioned a price but completely skipped the back-office framing AND silence.
- 0 = Never anchored.

#### Negotiation
- 10 = Asked seller's best number with full value reset (as-is, no commission, no fees, closing costs, timeline). Pulled a number.
- 7 = Asked but value reset was incomplete.
- 5 = Asked for their best but skipped the value bullets.
- 3 = Negotiation became back-and-forth on price without strategic moves.
- 0 = Never gave seller a chance to counter — went straight to closing.

#### Trial Close 2
- 10 = Conditional framing ("if I can get this approved, you ready to move forward right?") + verbal YES.
- 7 = Asked but yes was qualified ("I think so").
- 5 = Asked something close but no real commitment surfaced.
- 3 = Vague hint at move-forward without clear ask.
- 0 = Skipped.

#### Second Hold
- 10 = Real hold; clear strategic decision (cash vs novation) on return.
- 7 = Hold but cash-vs-novation choice was telegraphed before the hold.
- 5 = Brief hold; came back without clear plan.
- 3 = Perfunctory hold; wasted.
- 0 = No second hold.

#### Approval / Close
- 10 = "Approved for our portfolio" framing with excitement; clean walk-through of email/doc steps; signature secured OR firm sign window committed.
- 7 = Excitement framing OK but doc walkthrough rushed.
- 5 = Got approval words out but seller hesitated and rep didn't recover well.
- 3 = Brief mention of approval; no signature, no firm next step.
- 0 = No approval framing OR no signature/commitment.

## PART 6 — RED FLAGS (AUTO-DEDUCTIONS)

These specific behaviors automatically reduce the relevant step's score, regardless of how well the rest of the step went:

- **Rep gives a price without going to hold first** → Anchor and/or Negotiation lose 2-3 points.
- **Rep says "I'm not going to lowball you" / "I don't want to insult you"** → primes the seller to expect lowball; -2 to Anchor.
- **Rep argues with the seller** ("Zillow is wrong," etc.) → -2 to whichever step the argument occurred in.
- **Rep gives the seller homework** ("go check Zillow," "find your tax bill") → -2 to whichever step.
- **Rep over-promises** ("we can definitely close in 7 days") → -2 to that step.
- **Rep specifies dollar repair costs** ("the roof needs $15,000") → -2 to Negotiation.
- **Rep doesn't pause after the anchor** → Anchor capped at 5 regardless of other quality.
- **Rep accepts a "maybe" as a yes at any Trial Close** → that Trial Close capped at 5.
- **Rep names a competitor by name** ("we're better than Opendoor") → -2 to that step.
- **Rep talks more than the seller during the Motivation step** → Motivation capped at 5.
- **Rep brings up the price first (before asking the seller's asking price)** → -3 to Asking Price.

## PART 7 — REFERENCE EXAMPLES

(Concrete examples of what each grade looks like for the steps reps most often misjudge.)

### Anchor — 10/10 example
Rep (downward inflection): "So when I was back there, I was peeking over one of the ladies' shoulders, and it looks like they're coming back somewhere between $135,000 and $145,000…"
(5-second silence)
Seller: "What?? That's way too low!"
Rep (calm): "Oh, I'm not saying that's what we're going to pay you — that's just what I was seeing back there."
Seller: "Oh, ok."

→ **Why 10:** Range, not a single number. Framed as estimate FROM back-office (not from rep). Downward inflection. Held silence. Handled pushback without backpedaling on the framing — only on the literalness.

### Anchor — 3/10 example
Rep: "We can probably do around $140,000 for it."

→ **Why 3:** Stated as the offer, not as a back-office estimate. No range. No pause. No framing. Direct from rep — kills authority.

### Trial Close 1 — 10/10 example
Rep: "So if we can get to a price that works for you AND works for our back office, is this something you want to move forward with today?"
Seller: "Yes."

→ **Why 10:** Conditional framing forces seller to commit on the IF. Got a clean verbal yes.

### Trial Close 1 — 5/10 example
Rep: "So would you sell to us if the price was right?"
Seller: "I mean, maybe, depends on the number."

→ **Why 5:** No conditional framing. Question too open. "Maybe" is a soft non-commit but rep didn't push for a real yes.
